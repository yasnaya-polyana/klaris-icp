# Klaris — Economic Buyer Playbook

Motion: founder-led, £15–60k ACV. Data: public web + LinkedIn Sales Navigator.

At this ACV you are looking for **one budget holder plus one champion**, not a
buying committee. The job is to identify the person who can say yes without
convening anyone, and the person who will make the case for them.

---

## Buyer archetypes — the buyer moves with company shape

### A. 30–80 people, VC-backed, pre-first-CE-mark
- **Economic buyer:** CEO or COO.
- **Champion:** the single RA person, or an external RA consultant.
- **Why:** the CE mark *is* the next fundraise milestone. RA has no independent
  budget; the CEO is personally exposed to a delayed approval.
- **Sell:** time-to-revenue and de-risked timeline. **Never sell compliance.**
  "Six weeks off your submission" beats "fewer gaps" every time.
- **Watch:** may have no technical file yet — check the `technical_file_exists`
  gate carefully here.

### B. 80–250 people, commercial, multi-product  ← *core ICP*
- **Economic buyer:** VP / Head of Regulatory Affairs, or VP Quality &
  Regulatory. Has a real, defensible budget line.
- **Champion:** RA Manager or Senior RA Specialist — the person who actually
  assembles the technical file and eats the deficiency letter.
- **Likely blocker:** Head of Quality, if they read Klaris as a threat to the
  QMS they own. Neutralise early: Klaris reviews, it does not draft, and it
  does not replace the QMS.
- **Sell:** deficiency-letter avoidance and RA team capacity.

### C. 250–400 people
- **Economic buyer:** COO / CTO / Chief Medical Officer; RA and QA report up.
- **Champion:** Director of RA.
- **New friction:** procurement, InfoSec/DPIA review, vendor onboarding.
- **Sell:** portfolio-wide consistency and permanent audit readiness.

## Twelve ways to find the economic buyer

Ordered by signal strength. The first two are the highest-leverage and the
least-used.

### 1. Find the PRRC
EU MDR **Article 15** requires every manufacturer to designate, **by name**, a
*Person Responsible for Regulatory Compliance* who carries **personal legal
accountability** for the conformity of technical documentation before release.

That is precisely what Klaris checks. The PRRC is the highest-intent named
individual in the European medtech market, and almost nobody prospects for them.

Where to find them:
- LinkedIn / Sales Nav title search: `PRRC`, `Person Responsible for Regulatory
  Compliance`, often appended to another title ("QA Manager & PRRC").
- The company's compliance or quality page.
- EUDAMED actor registration data.
- The Declaration of Conformity, which often names the signatory.

The PRRC is usually the **champion**, sometimes the **economic buyer** in
archetype B. Either way they are the highest-conviction first conversation.

### 2. PRRC turnover is a screaming trigger
A new PRRC inherits **personal legal liability for documents they did not
write**. Their rational first act is an independent gap assessment of the
inherited technical file. Track PRRC title changes via Sales Nav job-change
alerts. This is close to a perfect buying moment.

### 3. Budget-authority triangulation — infer authority, don't trust titles
Titles lie, especially in 30–400 person companies. Score three public tells:

1. Named on regulatory filings, certificates, or the Declaration of Conformity
2. Appears on the website leadership/team page
3. Posted or is named as hiring manager on the RA/QA job req

**Two of three ⇒ economic buyer, high confidence.**

Tell 3 is the most underused: **whoever posted the RA job ad is almost always
the RA budget owner.** Someone who can hire a £70k FTE can approve £15–60k of
software.

### 4. Job ads hand you the level above, for free
Nearly every RA job ad states "reports to [title]". That is your economic buyer,
named by level, without any research. The same ad also gives you team size,
tool stack, and the current pain in the company's own words — quote it back.

### 5. Declarations of Conformity are a free org chart
DoCs are frequently published as PDFs and are signed with a name and title.
`site:company.com "declaration of conformity" filetype:pdf`.

### 6. The interim-RA rate as price anchor
A company advertising **contract or interim RA at £600–900/day** has already
unlocked £70–110k of discretionary spend for six months of one person's time.
That is public, and it reframes Klaris at £15–60k as obviously cheap.
Use their own day rate as the anchor in the first email.

### 7. Investor backdoor
Systematically enumerate the portfolios of **Meridian Health Ventures** (Klaris's
lead — itself backed by Guy's & St Thomas', King's College Hospital, UCLH and
Cedars-Sinai) and **Antler**, plus co-investors on the pre-seed. Every medtech
portfolio company is a warm intro, and the health-system LPs open
hospital-adjacent networks that cold outreach cannot reach.

### 8. Timeline-slip inference
Diff a target's product/regulatory pages against the Wayback Machine. A CE-mark
or launch date that quietly moves is almost always a Notified Body deficiency
round in progress. Public, legal, and very high signal — and it tells you they
are in pain *right now*, before they have admitted it publicly.

### 9. The "second submission" watch
An FDA K-number that goes quiet and then reappears is a rejected first attempt.
These companies just personally lived the 69% statistic in Klaris's own pitch.
Lead with it.

### 10. Two-sided job-change tracking
A Head of RA who changes employer is *two* opportunities: a warm re-sell at the
new company (they arrive with a mandate and a budget), and a capacity vacuum at
the old one. Sales Nav alerts cover both sides; most people only work one.

### 11. Conference speaker circuits
RAPS Euro Convergence, TOPRA Annual Symposium, MedTech Summit, MedtecLIVE,
Notified Body seminars. Speaker lists are public, name senior RA leaders with
their companies, and anyone speaking on MDR documentation burden has
pre-qualified themselves.

### 12. Consultancy channel inversion
RA consultancies prepare technical files for many small manufacturers. They are
simultaneously a competitor and the fastest distribution channel in the market —
one consultant adopting Klaris brings ten accounts behind them.

**Flagged, not built.** This needs its own segment model (different economics,
different buyer, likely different pricing) and should not be mixed into the
direct-manufacturer score. Recommend as ICP v2.

## Outreach sequencing once the buyer is identified

1. **Champion first** in archetype B/C — the RA Manager or PRRC. They will tell
   you the real state of the technical file and who signs.
2. **Economic buyer directly** in archetype A — the CEO. There is no one else.
3. Lead with the **specific trigger you found**, not with the product. "I saw
   your Head of RA req has been open since May" earns a reply; "we do
   AI-powered compliance" does not.
4. Anchor on **their** number: the interim day rate, the NB fee, or the cost of
   a six-month delay to the launch date they published.
